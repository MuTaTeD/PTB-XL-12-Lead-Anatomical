Two things to fix before this goes out
1. A likely genuine data error, not just a polish item. Table 4 (cyclic 10-fold) reports Model 2 and Model 3 with the identical mean ROC-AUC of 0.9407 (± 0.0056 vs ± 0.0051). This directly conflicts with the paper's own Wilcoxon claim two paragraphs later that Model 3 "significantly outperforms" Model 2 (p=0.0039) — you can't have a significant paired difference with identical means. This reads like a copy-paste artifact from editing one row and not the other (earlier drafts had Model 2=0.9295, Model 3=0.9329 in this same table). This needs to be corrected and re-verified against the actual per-fold results before submission — it's exactly the kind of internal-consistency slip a careful reviewer catches in the first pass.
2. The robustness suite contains a real, unflagged failure mode. Table 10 shows that at severe noise (SNR=0dB), Model 3 collapses from 0.81 to 0.5763 AUC — actually worse than Model 1's 0.8146 at the same condition (Δ = −0.2384). Similarly at the highest baseline-wander severity, Model 3 underperforms Model 1 (Δ = −0.0390). The abstract and body only surface the favorable robustness numbers (missing leads, occluded territories), and this catastrophic-noise result isn't discussed anywhere in the Discussion or Limitations. Given this is the exact experiment the advisor asked for, presenting it in the table but not engaging with the unflattering result in the text will likely read as selective reporting to a careful reviewer — which would undercut the credibility you've built everywhere else in this draft. I'd recommend adding a sentence or two to the Discussion/Limitations: something like "under extreme uniform noise, territory-dropout's reliance on self-sufficient per-branch features appears to degrade more sharply than the flat baseline's pooled representation, suggesting the robustness benefit is specific to structured missing-data patterns rather than generic signal degradation" — turning a hidden gap into an honest, interesting finding, which is consistent with how well the rest of this draft handles unflattering results (the STTC zero-shot weakness, the LMI/ALMI split, the untouched zero-shot F1).

* Figure 5's occlusion/attention divergence — disclosed honestly but only explained via one case; worth a one-line cohort-level check ("across all N=550 MI cases, cross-territory attention assigns >X% to Inferior leads in Y% of anterior-MI cases, consistent with reciprocal change") if the data's already sitting there from the per-patient audit.
