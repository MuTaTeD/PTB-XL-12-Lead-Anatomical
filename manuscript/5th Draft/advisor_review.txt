My assessment: major revision. The paper presents a promising compact ECG classifier, but its current evidence does not establish state-of-the-art performance or clinically validated explanations. The strongest contribution is the combination of anatomical grouping, territory dropout, and an organized attribution display.
I reviewed the manuscript and checked relevant original research and benchmark sources. This assessment evaluates the reported evidence; it does not independently reproduce the experiments.
1.	The main SOTA comparison contains a task mismatch.
Table 7 reports 0.925 AUC for both the published ResNet and Inception baselines. However, the original authors’ repository assigns those values to the all-statements task, whereas this manuscript predicts five diagnostic superclasses.
The relevant published superclass results are:
Model	Five-superclass macro AUC	Evaluation
ResNet1D-Wang	0.930	Standard held-out Fold 10
XResNet1D101	0.928	Standard held-out Fold 10
Inception1D	0.921	Standard held-out Fold 10
Proposed model	0.9329	Mean across ten cyclic test folds
These benchmark values and the standard split are documented in the original repository. GitHub (https://github.com/helme/ecg_ptbxl_benchmarking)
The apparent advantage over the strongest baseline listed above is only 0.0029 AUC, and even that difference cannot establish superiority because the evaluation protocols differ. Table 7 needs rebuilding with matching tasks, dataset versions, splits, and F1 definitions. Its cautionary footnote does not resolve this mismatch.
2.	The closest recent methods are missing from the novelty discussion.
Relevant technique	Why it matters to this paper
Leadwise clustering multi-branch network, 2024	Already combines medical knowledge, lead grouping, and multiple branches. Anatomical decomposition cannot be presented as broadly new without comparison.
TolerantECG, 2025	Explicitly evaluates missing leads, noise, and their combination—directly relevant to the proposed robustness claim.
ACL-ECG, 2026	Incorporates anatomical relationships and physiology-aware augmentation into contrastive learning, directly overlapping the paper’s motivation.
ECGFounder-PT, 2025 preprint	Investigates modern pretraining and stochastic-depth regularization, relevant to the claim that structured dropout provides a distinctive advance.
3.	These approaches are documented in their original publications. ScienceDirect (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1350453324000973)
4.	They are necessary comparators, not automatically superior models. For example, TolerantECG reports 0.926 superclass AUC on its original-signal evaluation, but its training and robustness protocol differs. ECGFounder-PT reports other label granularities, so its headline AUCs should not be compared directly with this paper’s five-class result. arxiv.org (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09887v1)
5.	The experiments do not isolate whether anatomy causes the improvement.
Model 3 improves over Model 2 by 0.0034 AUC, supporting a possible benefit from territory dropout. However, both already use anatomical grouping.
The essential missing comparison is random lead groups of the same sizes, with identical branches and dropout. Also needed are ordinary lead dropout, generic branch dropout, and a flat model matched for parameter count.
Without these controls, the gain could reflect regularization or architectural changes rather than the proposed anatomical organization. Having fewer parameters than Model 1 does not prove that anatomy explains the improvement.
6.	The statistical evidence is too limited for a superiority claim.
Section 4.1 calls the ten test folds independent, but their fitted models share substantial training data. A Wilcoxon test across these results therefore needs a dependence caveat.
Report the ten paired results, exact p-value, confidence interval for the performance difference, and repeated training seeds. Seed 42 alone cannot establish training stability. Add a standard Fold 10 comparison with patient-level paired bootstrap intervals, keeping all tuning within training/validation data.
7.	External generalization is uneven, and the label mapping needs verification.
Table 6 reports:
External class	AUC	F1
NORM	0.9049	0.5816
CD	0.8462	0.8306
STTC	0.6036	0.3229
The weak STTC result materially limits the claim of robust external transfer. Moreover, this evaluation covers three classes, leaving MI and HYP externally unvalidated.
Publish the complete SNOMED-to-superclass mapping, particularly how 4,988 records become CD-positive. The manuscript does not provide enough information to verify that target-label meanings match PTB-XL.
Explain “Tuned F1”: if thresholds were selected using CPSC labels, that F1 is not a fully untouched zero-shot evaluation. This is an ambiguity requiring clarification, not evidence that leakage occurred.
8.	The anatomical interpretation exceeds the available ground truth.
The description of four “mutually orthogonal coronary vascular beds” is inaccurate. These are useful lead groups, but not independent coronary compartments; aVR is not a separate coronary vascular bed.
More seriously, SCP infarct-location labels do not establish angiographically verified culprit arteries. Inferior infarction can involve RCA or LCx depending on coronary anatomy. ahajournals.org
Replace “coronary culprit ground-truth validation” with agreement with ECG-derived infarct-location annotations. Claims about acute infarction, arterial occlusion, and catheterization decisions require additional clinical evidence.
The lateral subgroup also needs honest treatment: 21.31% lateral versus 62.13% antero-septal attribution is not straightforward evidence of successful lateral localization. Separate LMI from ALMI and report predefined patient-level localization accuracy.
9.	The XAI checks are useful, but do not establish clinical faithfulness.
The manuscript deserves credit for including parameter-randomization checks and baseline sensitivity analysis. However:
o	Integrated Gradients completeness checks whether attributions account for a model-score difference; it does not establish correct clinical reasoning. proceedings.mlr.press
o	A single shared territory-attention vector is not automatically a class-specific explanation.
o	Zeroing input leads during explanation differs from dropping branch features during training.
o	Territory-average attribution cannot demonstrate precise ST-segment, J-point, or QRS localization.
o	Grouped explanations can also be produced by aggregating attributions from a flat model.
Compare the same XAI methods across flat and anatomical models, using clinician-marked waveform regions, perturbation faithfulness, and failure cases. The current comparison with generative counterfactuals is largely conceptual; it does not demonstrate empirical superiority over them.
10.	There are concrete figure and implementation inconsistencies.
Figure 3, page 9: the caption claims three models on Fold 10, but the figure contains only two model rows, and the upper row is labelled Fold 8. The displayed totals also differ: the upper NORM matrix sums to 2,176, while the lower sums to 2,198. This must be corrected before the comparison is interpretable.
Figure 5, page 16: anterior territory occlusion is larger, but the attention bars favor inferior leads—52.3% inferior versus 40.0% antero-septal. Section 5.1 nevertheless describes attention as anterior-dominant. Report this disagreement rather than claiming concordance.
Table 2 includes attention fusion, while Algorithm 1 omits it; Figure 2 also depicts a different pooling/SE sequence. Provide one definitive architecture and executable configuration.
11.	The robustness and deployment claims need direct tests.
Cross-dataset transfer alone does not prove elimination of shortcuts or vendor-noise dependence. Test missing territories, individual missing leads, baseline wander, electrode-motion noise, and lead swaps across controlled severity levels.
Report calibration and explanation latency as well. Near-100% sigmoid outputs should not be presented as reliable clinical confidence without calibration evidence.
The most defensible revision is to position this as a compact, anatomically grouped classifier with preliminary explanation validation. Before resubmission, prioritize correcting Table 7 and Figure 3, adding matched anatomical-versus-random grouping controls, auditing external labels and thresholds, and replacing clinical certainty claims with conclusions supported by the actual tests.
